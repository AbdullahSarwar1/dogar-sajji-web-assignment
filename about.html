Born in Balochistan, Perfected for You

Dogar Sajji originated in 1998 when our grandfather, Haji Abdul Rasheed Dogar, traveled across the rugged landscapes of Sibi and Quetta to apprentice under traditional nomad grill masters. He returned with an uncompromising commitment to the ancient art of slow-cooking meat on upright wooden skewers arranged in circular open-fire pits.

Unlike commercial fast food or heavily sauced barbecues, authentic Sajji relies purely on coarse Himalayan rock salt, a subtle citrus marinade, and the transformative radiant heat of seasoned wood charcoal.

“True Sajji cannot be rushed with gas flames or electric ovens. It asks for dry wood embers, pure salt, and the patience to watch every drop of flavor lock inside.” — Haji Abdul Rasheed Dogar, Founder

Now in our third generation, we continue that very same ritual each afternoon in Karachi. Every skewer is balanced by hand and watched attentively until the skin is crisped to perfection while the meat stays tender, succulent, and fragrant.

Traditional Sajji does not use heavy powdered red spices, artificial food coloring, or yogurt pastes. Instead, the meat is lightly rubbed with natural rock salt and freshly extracted raw papaya, then roasted whole on skewers beside burning embers. The flavor comes entirely from the caramelization of the meat’s natural juices and the aromatic wood smoke.
A whole chicken Sajji takes approximately 90 to 110 minutes of gentle, continuous turning over embers. Mutton leg Sajji takes upwards of 2.5 hours. We keep batches rotating continuously during dining hours so guests receive freshly finished cuts within 15 to 20 minutes of ordering.
